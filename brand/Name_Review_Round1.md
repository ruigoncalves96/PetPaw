## The two that survive

### Mieli
- **Sound:** all soft letters (m, l) and open vowels. Exactly the "warm, soft, round" word the brief asks for.
- **Meaning:** Finnish *mind*. Close to Italian *miele* (honey), and *mieli* is in fact its plural. Polish *mieli* means "they had", which is neutral. The PT and EN meanings are empty, so the brand defines it.
- **Risks:**
  - **Pronunciation is uncertain in English** (mee-EL-ee? MEE-lee? MY-lee?). PT/ES/IT readers will say *mi-É-li*.
  - **MIELI Mental Health Finland** is a large national charity (founded 1897). It's a different category, but health services fall in class 44, the same class as veterinary services.
  - **It looks like *Miele*** (added in round 2), the big German appliance brand, which has its own smart-home app. The difference is one letter, which could cause visual confusion.
- **Test:** ask 5 people (PT, EN, ES, FR, DE speakers) to read it aloud without hearing it first, then ask what it makes them feel.

### Arca
- **Meaning:** *arca* is "ark" in PT/ES/IT/Latin. *Arca de Noé* is the story of **no animal left behind**, which is exactly our soul ("No animal forgotten") and "every species equally". *Arca* also means a chest for keeping precious things safe (memories, privacy).
- **Sound:** open "a" vowels, but the hard middle "c" makes it firmer and less round than Mieli.
- **Risks:**
  - **Crowded name:** several apps are already called Arca (a task manager, Arca.so, a smart-button app…), plus NYSE Arca and Arca in crypto.
  - **Too close to Arc**, the browser you named as a reference. It could look derivative.
  - *Arca de Noé* is a very common name for pet shops and vet clinics in PT/BR. That helps it feel familiar but makes it hard to own (classes 35 / 44).
  - A mild religious association.
- **Workarounds:**
  - Use a store name with a descriptor ("Arca — Animal Care").
  - Try a variant spelling or form (*Arka*, *Arcaia*, *Arcadia*, which is taken a lot…). Variants need their own check.

## What your list says about your taste
Five of the six are **two syllables, end in a vowel, and use soft consonants** (m, l, n). Only Kin is the exception. That pattern, plus a meaning like Arca's (shelter, keeping, family, every animal), is the brief for the next longlist.
