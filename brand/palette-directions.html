Four colour directions for an every-animal care app

Each direction is shown as a palette with contrast checks, then applied to the same "Today" screen in light and dark. The pets are a dog, a leopard gecko, a cockatiel and a rabbit, so you can judge whether a colour feels right for all of them. The fonts are placeholders; typography is the next round.

What the palette has to do

  • Feel trustworthy enough to hold vaccine and medication records
  • Feel warm enough that people open it every day
  • Fit a gecko or a horse as naturally as a dog
  • Leave room for status colours (overdue, due soon, done)

What it should avoid

  • Clinical hospital blue-and-white
  • Toy-like rainbow palettes that read as childish
  • Paw-print orange, which signals "dogs and cats"
  • Looks that feel generated, which hurt trust (a PetNoter review complained about AI art)

How to judge them

  • Look at the phones before the swatches
  • Ask: would I trust this with my pet's health history?
  • Check the dark version too, since many people use it at night
  • Click any swatch to copy its hex value
Comparison

How the four directions compare

These scores are my judgement against the brief, out of 5. Treat them as a starting point for your own reaction and for feedback from people in the survey.

My recommendation: Moss & Marigold, with Plum & Apricot as the alternative

Moss ties every species together through habitat (garden, terrarium, meadow, stable), which supports "every animal" better than any single-animal cue. It reads calm and dependable for records, and the marigold accent keeps it warm. Plum & Apricot is the most distinctive and the most likely to appeal to younger exotic keepers, but it signals health and reliability less. Show both to 5–10 people before deciding.

Shared layers

Colours that work with any direction

Two functional sets sit on top of the brand palette. They stay the same whichever direction you pick, so people learn them once.

Care status

Every status always pairs its colour with a word or icon, so it still reads for colour-blind users and where it shares a hue with the brand (for example "done" green next to Moss).

Species tags

A soft tint per species group, for avatars, filters and charts. They're generated from one rule (same lightness, different hue), so adding a group later stays consistent.

Next

How to choose

  1. Shortlist one or two directions. Mixing is fine, for example Moss with Plum's apricot accent.
  2. Show the phone mockups to 5–10 people, including at least two exotic keepers. Ask which one they'd trust with their pet's health records and which feels right for their animal.
  3. Typography round: pick a heading and body pair that matches the chosen direction.
  4. Once the name is chosen: the wordmark and app icon, tested at small sizes on both stores.
  5. Then turn the result into a design system (tokens, components, light and dark) that the app build uses directly.
